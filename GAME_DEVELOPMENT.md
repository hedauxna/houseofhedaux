# Games Room Life Sim

The life sim is a browser-only prototype available on the live site. In the Games Room, choose **A Day in the Life** to open it. It runs in the browser and does not send game state to a server.

## Run it locally

In VS Code:

1. Open the `HouseOfHedaux2.0` repository folder.
2. Install the **Live Server** extension if it is not already installed.
3. In Explorer, right-click `index.html` and choose **Open with Live Server**.
4. Open the local URL shown by VS Code, usually <http://127.0.0.1:5500/>.

Use the local server to test changes before publishing. Keep the server running while testing; a browser `ERR_CONNECTION_REFUSED` means the server is stopped or you opened the wrong port.

Alternatively, open a terminal in the repository folder and run `py -m http.server 8000`, then open <http://localhost:8000/>. This requires the Python Launcher for Windows. Stop either server with `Ctrl+C` in its terminal.

The simulation starts on **Monday 4 January 2027**, aged 21, and runs for 365 days through 3 January 2028. No birthday has been specified, so age remains 21. The date and day number appear alongside the current phase. Each day has eight stages: **Early Morning**, **Morning Routine**, **Morning Activity**, **Daytime Routine**, **Daytime Activity**, **Evening Routine**, **Evening Activity**, and **Night**. Choose one option per stage. Morning Routine offers breakfast, hygiene, exercise and other ways to begin the day; choosing a non-meal option skips breakfast. Lunch is chosen during Daytime Routine, while Evening Routine offers dinner or hygiene. **Eat Out** is an Evening Activity and counts as dinner if it was missed during the routine; choosing **Skip Dinner** still applies its immediate penalties. Morning and Daytime Activity each offer work, study, socialising, chores and hobbies. Evening activities include overtime after a shift for the current job, entertainment, study and social choices.

Staying in bed skips both Morning stages and breakfast. **Full Self Care Day** is a Morning Routine choice that skips breakfast and both activity slots, including work, while preserving lunch and the evening; normal period Hunger losses still apply. **Sleep Early** is an Evening Activity that skips the Night choice and advances to the next day. Other Evening choices lead to **Go To Bed** or **Stay Up Late**. Their base Energy recovery is 80, 60 and 20 respectively, adjusted by Stress, Hunger and Fitness and capped at 100. Staying up late also costs 14 Energy the following morning, but may lead to a creative breakthrough or a rare paid commission. The 365th day ends the year.

The Current Account starts at £500, with the overdraft and available funds shown directly below it. Starting stats are Health 95, Energy 100, Fitness 75, Hunger 50, Happiness 75, Stress 50, Confidence 0, Intelligence 10, Hygiene 80 and Home Cleanliness 100. Fitness declines by 5 at the end of each day. Gym visits and intense workouts add 15 Fitness, and strolls add 7; some sedentary activities reduce it. Fitness influences job application chances and gym gains; below 30 it reduces Energy recovery and adds 2 daily Health deterioration, while above 80 it improves Energy recovery and adds 1 daily Health. The daily health effects use Fitness before that day's decay. Hunger is a visible 0–100 meter where 100 means well nourished; it falls by 10 in the morning, 15 in Daytime, 15 in the evening, and 10 at night. Meals restore Hunger. Low Hunger reduces sleep recovery, mood, study gains and application chances. Stat colors adapt to each value, with Stress using an inverse scale.

Home Cleanliness falls by 5 each day. Household Chores can be selected in either activity slot to restore 50, at an Energy and money cost. After daily decay, a home below 30 increases Stress and reduces Happiness; below 15 these effects are stronger. A warning appears below 20.

Applications can offer entry-level or more advanced careers according to attributes, skills, and qualifications. Unsuccessful applications display a temporary notification and remain in Notable events. Working a shift improves that role's skill; study and reading build technical skill, and workouts build fitness skill. Skills and qualifications stay with the player when changing jobs and appear under Milestones. Study can unlock the Study Skills Certificate required by some professional roles. Choose **Quit Job** in Employment to leave immediately; apply again during a Morning or Daytime Activity. Quitting resets the new role's promotion progress, not earned skills or achievements.

Jobs list workdays, working periods, annual salary, pay frequency and requirements in offers and Employment. Most roles schedule separate Morning and Daytime shifts; Night Security works Evening and Night, with an optional sleep after a night shift. Retail, café, care, nursing, emergency and security roles have fixed patterns that can include weekends. During a scheduled period, only **Go To Work** and **Report An Absence** appear initially. Skipping a period without reporting, including by staying in bed, choosing self-care or sleeping early, counts as a missed shift. An offer accepted after a period has passed does not create an absence for that period.

Employment displays attendance, performance and qualitative employer feedback. Attendance is worked shifts divided by expected shifts, excluding reported absences. **Report An Absence** offers doctor or dentist appointments, illness, a mental health day, family emergency, personal day or another reason; normal activities then become available for that period. Reported absences are self-reported and unpaid for now, pending future approval and leave-entitlement systems. A matching doctor or dentist visit provides an appointment record. Resting during illness and self-care during a mental health day support credibility; a clearly inconsistent activity may reduce it slightly. Other reasons and ambiguous activities are treated neutrally.

Employer trust, credibility and HR concern are hidden and shown only as qualitative summaries. Frequent reported absences are counted by day and can lead to monitoring; supported absences alone cannot result in formal discipline. Missed unreported shifts and repeated questionable behaviour increase concern. Responses progress from check-ins and support to warnings and, only for sustained concerning behaviour, dismissal, with at least seven days between steps. Working rebuilds trust and reduces concern. Hidden performance changes with attendance, wellbeing, job-specific performance, qualifications and employer trust. Every seven days, a deterministic review promotes eligible employees: more than 30 days in the current role, over 90% attendance, over 75 performance, at least 20 relevant skill, the job's requirements and sufficient employer trust and credibility without serious HR concern. Each of the two promotions changes the title and raises annual salary by 25% of the original. Time in role resets after promotion.

Shifts accrue gross wages rather than paying immediately. Warehouse, retail and café roles pay weekly on Friday; delivery, emergency and night-security roles pay fortnightly on alternating Fridays; other roles pay on their last scheduled working day of the month. Office Junior starts at £24,000 per year. Each pay period's salary is divided across its scheduled shifts, so joining partway through or missing a shift reduces that payday's earnings. Overtime accrues 1.5 times a regular period's gross wages. A payday card shows gross pay, estimated tax, National Insurance and net pay credited to the current account; net pay is included in weekly and monthly reports. The simplified deductions use a £12,570 annual allowance, then 20% tax and 8% National Insurance on the remainder; they are game estimates, not tax advice. Earned wages remain payable on the normal payday after quitting or dismissal.

On Sunday evening, a reflection summarises income, spending, wellbeing changes, achievements, relationships, qualifications, promotions and significant events. Each regular reflection covers the period since the previous Sunday evening; Sunday night belongs to the following reflection. **Sleep Early** closes the day before displaying its reflection. At the end of the run, a final partial-week reflection is shown.

Household bills accrue privately until the last day of the calendar month. Monthly rent is £450, with base electricity £30, water £20, internet £25 and subscriptions £12; the first and final partial months are prorated by days played. Showers and baths add water usage; cooking, TV and hobbies add electricity usage. Using the gym activates a £30 membership for that month only, unless a free-month encounter waives it. Groceries, eating out, transport, social activities, courses and other purchases are paid immediately. Hygiene and gym usage are no longer charged immediately. The finance report shows income, expenses, net profit or loss, the current balance, and any unpaid bills. It also appears for the final partial month at year end.

The current account permits an overdraft down to −£500; purchases cannot exceed available funds. Each month, 2% interest is charged on the negative balance and unpaid bills. Bills that exceed the available overdraft are carried forward as arrears and repaid from available funds at the next report; they are not discarded. Debt adds Stress periodically, more so for larger amounts, but reaching the overdraft limit does not itself end the game. Hidden appearance, dental care, mental wellbeing, weight and household maintenance influence small aspects of play and issue occasional reminders. Haircuts, dental check-ups, self-care, exercise and chores can help maintain them.

Routines, activities, jobs, random encounters, and daily risks are defined in data arrays in `games/life-sim.js`. Jobs have workdays, working periods, pay frequency, annual salary, stress cost, promotion path, qualifications and performance attributes. Regular period Energy costs split the job's original daily cost across its working periods; overtime costs 8 more than that original cost. New jobs may use conditions such as `{ all: [{ stat: "fitness", min: 60 }, { skill: "practical", min: 5 }] }` or qualification collection checks. Confidence and happiness can reduce work stress, while low energy, high stress, and consecutive workdays increase it.

Stress and hygiene influence recovery, confidence, social encounters, and job applications. Hygiene declines by 10 each day and by additional amounts for strenuous activities and work. Before each choice, Fitness, Hunger, Hygiene, Happiness and Home Cleanliness below 60, and Stress above 40, each add one quarter of a Health point in strain. Strain carries between choices and days; each four accumulated quarters removes 1 Health after the choice. The choice hint names the current causes and shows when strain will cause damage. This is in addition to the daily health deterioration from overlapping poor-hygiene, high-stress, low-energy, and skipped-meal conditions. Exercise gains fall when Energy is low, and repeated workouts add fatigue; repeated days at home reduce Confidence. Skipping two or more meals causes further penalties, while Hunger compounds the effects. Illness and unexpected expenses can also occur.

Health, Energy, Stress and Happiness can end a run. The game-over summary reports days survived, current day, money earned, longest job tenure, relationships, traits, achievements, and the reason the run ended. Restart Life begins a fresh simulation.

The dashboard uses three columns for the story and choices, character status and employment, and notable events, rare events, achievements, and milestones. On mobile landscape screens, choices appear on the left and status and events on the right. Study and reading appear first among regular activities, with doctor and dentist visits last; the remaining activities retain their relative order. Choice buttons show brief descriptions rather than numerical stat effects; outcomes are recorded in **Notable events**. Its banner shows the day, time, and phase; matching icons and subtle button accents distinguish periods. Warnings above the stats identify critical Health, Fitness, Energy, Hunger, and Stress. Rare-event cards remain visible for five in-game days; achievement progress and temporary green or red event notifications make important outcomes easier to spot. Routine and activity outcomes, job decisions, and random encounters appear in **Notable events**. Skipping a meal carries its usual penalties but has a small chance of a paid rare opportunity. Random encounters and daily risks are data-driven and conditional, so a walk, workout, study session, or social visit can lead to different results as character stats and the year progress. **Restart Year** resets the prototype.

The live site deploys from the `static-site-rollout` branch. Test changes locally, then commit and push them when you are ready for Render to publish the update.

For the focused browser regression checks, serve the repository locally and open `scripts/life-sim-tests.html`. The page reports whether calendar, schedules, finances, reminders and report transitions pass.

## Pit Wall

In the Games Room, choose **Pit Wall** and select **New Game** to draft a driver. Three distinct candidates are drawn at random from a pool of ten; choosing a card updates the Driver panel. Confirm a driver to draft a team from three candidates drawn from a pool of five. After confirming a team, choose from the 24 2027 weekends on the season calendar; all are selected by default. The calendar uses three columns, or two on narrow portrait screens to keep race names and dates visible. Confirm the season to open the first selected event. The calendar ordering and sprint designations follow the [2027 F1 calendar](https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2027) and [sprint list](https://www.astonmartinf1.com/en-GB/news/announcement/f1-2027-calendar-revealed).

Bahrain uses a sprint format and Saudi Arabia a standard format, each with five sessions. Every practice session runs for up to 60 simulated minutes, with **⏸**, **1x**, **10x** and **50x** controls. Pause freezes the session clock, AI and movement; choose a speed to resume. Practice starts with the player in the garage. Use the Command Centre to choose the next tyre and fuel load (10% to 100%, default Medium and 100%), then press **Send Out**. These controls remain available while circulating and prepare the next stint without changing the current one. The Timing panel shows the fitted compound, tyre condition and current fuel bars, fuel percentage and estimated remaining laps at the current mode, last and best laps, the countdown and speed. The estimate changes with fuel use, mode and fuel-efficiency upgrades; pending garage selections do not change the current reading until fitted. Tyre condition starts at 100% and decreases with wear. The newest-first lap history highlights the fastest lap in purple and scrolls when full. The Bahrain route displays team-coloured markers for all cars currently on track, with rings indicating their fitted tyre: red Soft, yellow Medium, white Hard, green Intermediate and blue Wet. Other circuits do not yet have practice movement routes.

Practice starts in Balanced driver mode. Use the **Command Centre** to select Push, Balanced or Manage: a full Push lap is 0.4 seconds faster than Balanced at the same conditions, at the cost of extra wear and fuel; Manage is 0.3 seconds slower but conserves both. Changes partway through a lap affect the remaining distance. Soft tyres are faster but wear faster; Hard tyres are slower but last longer. Intermediate and Wet currently behave like Medium. Wear slows the car, while a lighter fuel load improves pace and partly offsets it; wear can still make successive laps slower. Lap-to-lap variation is limited so these choices are easier to see. Reliability reduces both fast and slow deviations, improving consistency rather than guaranteeing a faster lap. Fuel consumption stops at zero; running out of fuel does not yet stop the car. Lap pace also reflects circuit length, driver and team traits, and active Handling, Aero, Energy Recovery, Track Evolution, Braking Reference Data and Overtaking Analysis upgrades; Tyre Insight and Fuel Mapping improve wear and fuel consumption.

During practice, the Driver panel shows a live best-lap classification for the player and nine fictional competitors, including the compound used for each driver's best lap. Driver, team, traits and upgrades are expanded beneath it, anchored at the bottom of the panel. The details area scrolls when the available height cannot accommodate all information, keeping classification visible. The final classification remains visible alongside the session review. AI drivers use the same lap model and run independent garage departures, stints, tyres, fuel loads and driver modes. Their Bahrain track markers match the initials and team colours in classification and disappear while in the garage. Hidden engine and testing programmes add uncertainty to their pace. Dry tyres are used for the current dry or unspecified conditions; intermediates require drizzle and a wet track, and wets require heavy rain and standing water. AI programme timings and variation are tunable in Developer Balance.

Use **Call To Pit** to request a return at the next lap boundary. The car enters the pit lane and its marker disappears while the 21.5-second base pit-entry countdown runs; effective Pit Efficiency upgrades reduce this time, with a five-second minimum. Session speed accelerates the countdown. The car then waits stationary in the garage until **Send Out** is pressed, which fits the pending tyre, resets wear and applies the pending fuel load. Garage waiting and pit entry do not count toward the next lap or consume tyres and fuel; the session clock and AI continue to run.

Sprint Qualifying is one 15-minute session, with the same garage, Command Centre, speeds and pit workflow. Each stint begins with an untimed **Out Lap** while the tyres warm; subsequent flying laps can set a best time. The fuel slider allows 0.1% increments and estimates the laps available at the selected driver mode and current fuel upgrades, including the out lap. Mode changes can alter the actual range. A flying lap without sufficient fuel is invalid and cannot set a time; the car does not yet stop when fuel runs out.

Qualifying AI make staggered, repeated low-fuel Push runs, favouring Soft tyres while also using other eligible compounds. The live classification shows each driver's best flying lap and its tyre. The final review shows the player's grid position, pole time and player best alongside the final classification; without a valid flying lap the player has no qualifying position. Sprint Qualifying determines the Sprint Race grid; ordinary Qualifying uses the same format and separately determines the Grand Prix grid. AI qualifying run timing, stint lengths, tyre weights and fuel reserve are tunable in Developer Balance.

Sprint Races cover at least 100 km, rounded up to whole laps. Choose starting tyres, fuel and driver mode before starting. The qualifying classification sets the grid; if qualifying is skipped, the player starts last behind the other drivers. Five lights illuminate at 0.6-second intervals, then go out after a random 1-2 second delay. All cars launch together. The **⏸**, **1x**, **10x** and **50x** controls affect race simulation, while the starting sequence uses real time and pauses when the session is paused.

The Driver panel ranks cars by track progress, so faster pace produces passes. AI use different compounds, fuel loads and modes, and some plan one tyre stop. **Call To Pit** changes to the selected next tyre after the pit-entry time; stops are optional and cannot add fuel. The starting-fuel control locks once the race begins. Its estimated remaining range reflects the current mode, and a fuel-short warning appears when that mode would not reach the finish. Cars retire when their fuel is exhausted. Bahrain uses its mapped route; other sprint circuits use a labelled schematic until their actual routes are available. AI strategy and starting-grid spacing are tunable in Developer Balance.

The Sprint Results review shows finishing position, best and average lap, laps completed and points earned. Finishers score 8-7-6-5-4-3-2-1 for the first eight places; retired cars score no points.

The Bahrain Grand Prix lasts 57 laps and uses the same grid, lights, fuel, tyre and pit controls as the Sprint Race. If Qualifying is skipped, the player starts last. Other circuits use their recorded lap count where available, or a distance of at least 305 km rounded up to whole laps. Grand Prix AI adjust modes to manage fuel and pit at varied tyre-wear thresholds; these thresholds and reserves are tunable in Developer Balance. Live classification shows estimated gaps and best laps. The race review includes finishing position, best and average lap, laps completed, points, pit stops and tyre usage. Final race classification stays in the Driver panel, while the Main panel shows Championship Standings at race review, weekend completion and season completion. Standings highlight the player and include a placeholder for race wins, which are not yet tracked. Finishers score 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 for the first ten places; retired cars score no points. Standings update immediately after both races and accumulate across the season. Cars share one marker renderer and cached route geometry to reduce per-frame work.

Practice ends when the countdown reaches zero or the player selects **Complete Session**. A session review shows lap data, duration and driver feedback. Complete at least three laps to choose an Engineering Discovery after the review; otherwise the session advances without a reward. Both qualifying sessions and both races offer a discovery after their reviews. Any session can be skipped without a reward before it starts. Each card independently rolls its rarity: before the Grand Prix, 45% green weekend discoveries, 45% blue common upgrades, 8% purple breakthroughs and 2% gold legendaries; after the Grand Prix, 90% blue, 8% purple and 2% gold. The cards in one offer are distinct. If a rarity has no eligible rewards, its weight is redistributed proportionally among the remaining rarities.

Green discoveries expire when the weekend ends; the other rarities last for the current game. Green and blue rewards are stackable, with their levels shown in the Driver panel; purple and gold rewards are unique per run unless explicitly marked stackable. Purple upgrades are three times the corresponding blue upgrade. Gold upgrades change future choices: Research Charter grants one full-offer reroll per session, Design Review grants one single-card reroll per session, and Legacy Lab adds another base increment to each level of existing and future blue upgrades. The Driver panel shows the resulting blue and purple effects, and the level separately when it differs from the effect. Rerolls exclude owned unique and maxed rewards, and cannot produce green after the Grand Prix. Upgrades and their effects are stored in memory and apply to the next simulated session. Starting a new game clears them.

Complete or skip the five sessions to proceed to the next selected round. After the last selected event, the season summary shows upgrade totals and championship position; weekend wins are not yet tracked. Bahrain and Saudi Arabia have circuit images and static weather; later weekends show circuit placeholders, with unavailable details marked TBC. Game state remains in memory.

The Command Centre starts expanded; use its arrow to collapse or expand it. The Games Room link in the console header returns to the games overview.

Circuit previews use display copies with transparent margins removed; the original supplied images are retained in `assets/`.

For Bahrain route inspection, open the site with `?pitWallDebug=1`, start a Bahrain session and use **Show Route**. Practice and qualifying start in the garage; races start on the grid. Showing the route pauses the session clock and markers, while **Resume Session** hides the overlay and resumes both. **Edit Route** enables dragging numbered waypoints, and **Export JSON** downloads the adjusted route; edits are in memory until exported and are not saved to the project automatically.

The same developer mode also exposes the collapsed **Balance** panel in the console header. It lists the simulation's base stats, traits, compounds, modes, fuel and pit effects, reward values and rarity weights. Each value is labelled **Simulated** or **Not Simulated**; newly registered values without a recognised simulation use are marked **Not Simulated**. Edit a value and leave its field to apply it; lap-target changes affect the next generated lap, while wear and fuel rates apply as the session advances. Values are in milliseconds, percentages or multipliers as labelled. **Export Balance Data** downloads the complete current configuration as JSON. Edits live only in the current console instance; reloading resets them to the defaults in `games/pit-wall-balance.js`, and exporting does not automatically import or persist them.

All new simulation tuning values must be defined in `DEFAULT_BALANCE` in `games/pit-wall-balance.js` and read from the active console's balance by the gameplay calculation, rather than duplicated as literals elsewhere. The developer panel and export automatically discover nested numeric, boolean and text values, including new categories and array entries; reopening the panel refreshes registrations. Update the coverage mapping in `games/pit-wall-balance-panel.js` when adding a simulation use, so it is labelled accurately; presentation metadata can also specify descriptive units and validation. Driver and team traits, vehicle stats, and stat-upgrade values must be registered before use; missing entries fail explicitly.

For focused browser regression checks, serve the repository locally and open `scripts/pit-wall-tests.html`.

## Keep secrets out of the frontend

There are no passwords or credentials in this game. HTML, CSS, and JavaScript sent to a browser are public, so never put a password, API key, or other secret in them.

The repository ignores `.env` files. If a future feature needs a secret, keep it in a server-side environment variable and read it from the Flask application; do not pass it into page markup or client-side JavaScript. Static browser code cannot safely hide environment variables.
